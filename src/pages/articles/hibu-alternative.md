If you're researching a Hibu alternative, you've probably already spotted the gap between what the platform promises and what it actually delivers. The pitch is convenience: one vendor handles your SEO, listings, and a basic website. The reality is generic creative, a proprietary ecosystem you can't easily leave, and limited visibility into what your ad spend is actually doing.

This guide covers the real alternatives available in 2026, what each one does well, where each one falls short, and how to figure out which approach fits your situation.

## Why Businesses Start Looking for a Hibu Alternative

Hibu's core appeal is simplicity. For some businesses, a single vendor managing their online presence is genuinely enough. But the complaints that surface consistently point to a few structural problems.

The platform holds a 2.5 out of 5 Trustpilot rating across 552 reviews — a meaningful signal when you're considering a long-term vendor relationship. The recurring frustrations: limited transparency on ad performance, contracts that are difficult to exit, and creative that looks identical to every other business in your category.

There's also a production gap worth understanding before you commit. Hibu doesn't produce original photography or video. Your ads and website run on stock imagery or whatever assets you hand over. When your competitors are using the same templated creative through the same platform, standing out becomes structurally difficult.

Ad spend and creative quality are directly connected. A Hibu alternative worth switching to needs to address that problem — not just move you to a different dashboard.

## What to Actually Compare When Evaluating Alternatives

Most local ad platforms compete hard on one or two dimensions while leaving others unaddressed. The right question isn't which platform is cheaper — it's which combination of services closes the gap between someone seeing your ad and becoming a booked customer.

A practical framework for comparison:

- **Creative production:** Does the vendor produce original photography and video, or do you supply it?
- **Ad management:** Is someone actively managing your Meta and Google campaigns, or is it self-serve?
- **Website and landing pages:** Do you own the asset, or does it live on the vendor's infrastructure?
- **Lead response:** What happens after someone fills out a form or calls and gets voicemail? Is there automated follow-up?
- **Integration:** Are these services connected in a coherent system, or are you managing five separate vendor relationships?

Run every alternative through this list. Most will fall short on at least two.

## The Main Alternatives and What They Actually Offer

### Scorpion

Scorpion builds genuinely high-quality custom websites and manages local advertising. The work is polished. But two things are worth knowing before you engage.

First, clients don't own the websites Scorpion builds. If you leave, you leave without the site. Second, reported retainer costs run $3,000 to $10,000 per month, with website builds ranging from $20,000 to $200,000. That's a significant commitment for an asset you won't walk away with.

Scorpion also produces no original brand media. Your ads run on whatever creative you already have.

### Thryv

Thryv is a SaaS platform, not a managed service. Published tiers run from $99 to $1,475 per month, covering CRM, scheduling, and basic online presence tools. What it doesn't do: creative production, ad management, or custom web design.

If you need a software layer to organize client communication, Thryv is worth a look. If you need someone to actually run your advertising and make your brand look credible, it's the wrong category of product.

### Townsquare Interactive

Townsquare Interactive serves more than 23,000 businesses with bundled website and SEO services. The scale is real, but the model is broad. There's no paid social management, no brand media production, and pricing transparency has been a recurring complaint in reviews.

It's a reasonable option if your primary need is a basic web presence and local SEO. It's not a fit if you want original creative or active campaign management.

### LocaliQ

Backed by Gannett, LocaliQ offers multi-channel ad management across search, display, and social. The platform has genuine reach and solid reporting infrastructure.

The limitation is the same as most managed ad vendors: LocaliQ relies on client-supplied creative. The media you bring is the media they run. If your photography and video are weak, the ad spend reflects that.

### Neuzenix

Neuzenix focuses on AI voice, chat, and lead response automation. Early reviews are strong. If your primary problem is inquiries falling through the cracks after hours or during busy periods, this is a focused solution worth evaluating.

What it doesn't cover: no creative production, no ad management, no website builds. It's a lead-response layer, not a full marketing system.

### LocalROI

LocalROI automates missed-call recovery and follow-up, specifically for home service businesses. It solves a real problem. But like Neuzenix, it's a narrow tool — no creative, no advertising, no web services. It works best as a component inside a broader system, not as a standalone Hibu replacement.

## How to Choose the Right Hibu Alternative for Your Business

The right answer depends on where your current setup is actually breaking down.

**If your ads are running but not converting,** the problem is usually creative or targeting. You need better photography and video, tighter audience definition, or both. A managed ad service that relies on your existing assets won't fix this.

**If your ads aren't running at all,** you need both creative and campaign management before anything else. Start there.

**If inquiries are coming in but not converting to booked jobs,** the issue is likely response speed and follow-up consistency. Automation tools like Neuzenix or LocalROI can help here — but only if the front-end demand is already working.

**If your website looks dated or doesn't turn visitors into inquiries,** no amount of ad spend will fix the underlying problem. A landing page rebuild or new site should come before you scale campaigns.

Most businesses in the $500K to $5M revenue range have gaps in more than one of these areas. Patching them with separate vendors creates coordination overhead and makes it hard to know which piece is actually working. When creative, ads, website, and follow-up are managed together, that ambiguity goes away.

## FAQs

**What are the main reasons businesses look for a Hibu alternative?**
The most common reasons are limited creative quality, low transparency on ad performance, difficulty exiting contracts, and a proprietary ecosystem that makes it hard to take your assets elsewhere. Hibu's 2.5 out of 5 Trustpilot rating across 552 reviews reflects these frustrations at scale.

**Does Hibu produce original photography or video for your ads?**
No. Hibu does not produce original brand media. Your ads and website use stock imagery or creative you supply, which limits how differentiated your brand can look compared to competitors on the same platform.

**What should I look for in a Hibu alternative?**
Look for a vendor that handles original creative production, active ad management, a website you actually own, and some form of lead follow-up. Most alternatives cover one or two of these areas. Few cover all four in a connected way.

**Is Scorpion a good Hibu alternative?**
Scorpion builds high-quality websites and manages local advertising, but clients don't own the sites Scorpion builds. Retainer costs are reported in the $3,000 to $10,000 per month range, and no original brand media is produced. It's a step up in quality but comes with meaningful trade-offs around ownership and cost.

**What does Thryv actually do?**
Thryv is a SaaS platform with published tiers from $99 to $1,475 per month. It handles CRM, scheduling, and basic online presence tools. It does not manage ads, produce creative, or build custom websites. It's a software tool, not a managed marketing service.

**Can I use multiple alternatives together instead of one vendor?**
Yes, and many businesses do. The trade-off is coordination overhead and the difficulty of knowing which piece is driving results. If you use separate vendors for ads, creative, and follow-up, you'll need a clear system for tracking where inquiries originate and what happens to them.

**What makes an integrated approach worth considering over separate vendors?**
When creative, advertising, and lead response operate inside the same framework, each piece informs the others. Ad performance data shapes creative decisions. Creative quality affects ad costs and conversion rates. Follow-up automation captures the inquiries that paid media generates. Separate vendors can replicate this, but it requires more active management from your side.
