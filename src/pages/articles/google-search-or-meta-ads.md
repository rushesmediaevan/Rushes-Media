Google Search is worth evaluating when people already search for the service you want to sell. Facebook and Instagram ads may be worth evaluating when showing the work helps people discover and consider it. The right starting point depends on your offer, audience, evidence and ability to turn an inquiry into business.

Neither choice guarantees useful leads. Start with one commercial question: **which service do we want to sell, to whom, and why would they choose us?** Then decide how to reach those people.

## First, be specific about what you are comparing

Google Search is one campaign type within Google Ads. Google also offers formats across other placements, including Display and YouTube. A proposal for “Google Ads” does not necessarily mean ads responding to a person's search. Google's [campaign-type guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2567043?hl=en) explains the distinction.

This guide compares Search with advertising on Facebook and Instagram. It is a planning framework, not a recommendation to activate either platform or spend a particular amount.

## When to investigate Google Search first

Look for evidence that people search for the specific service you can deliver in the area you serve. Useful inputs include customer language, relevant search queries already reaching your site and keyword research using the intended geography.

Check the meaning of those searches. Someone looking for a provider, someone researching a DIY project and someone looking for a job may use similar words. Your ad and destination need to address the right need.

Treat keyword estimates as clues. A zero for one narrow phrase does not establish that nobody wants the service. Explore related wording, geography and existing customer evidence before concluding there is no demand. Equally, a large estimate does not show that you can acquire customers profitably.

Ask the person proposing Search to explain which searches they want to reach, which they would exclude, and what visitors will find after the click.

## When to investigate Facebook and Instagram ads

Consider whether a photograph, demonstration or short video can make the value of your service clear to someone browsing. Use genuine work you have permission to show. A polished image with an unclear offer gives you little to learn from.

Do not assume every viewer is unfamiliar with your business or that Meta only serves awareness campaigns. People may already know you. Define the intended audience and action for the particular campaign rather than labeling all Meta traffic “low intent.”

The objective matters too. Meta's [campaign-objectives training](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/211544-ads-manager-objectives) connects the advertising objective to the business goal. Ask what the proposed campaign is trying to produce and how that action will be recorded. Traffic, inquiries and purchases are different outcomes.

## Compare what each option needs

**For Search:** relevant search demand, a clear service offer, appropriate geographic scope and a destination that answers the searcher's question.

**For Facebook and Instagram:** an audience hypothesis, a clear offer, suitable creative and an agreed action to measure. Establish whether you have enough approved material to test meaningfully.

**For both:** working contact tools, accurate measurement, a person responsible for inquiries and a budget the business can afford to evaluate. Neither platform repairs a broken form, unavailable capacity or an offer that nobody understands.

If the destination is the main problem, decide whether you need a [focused landing page or a full website](/articles/landing-page-or-full-website/) before adding more traffic.

## Fill in this brief before approving a test

Use your own records. Mark an unknown as unknown rather than filling it with an industry average.

1. **The work:** which service, customer and location are we trying to attract? What work should we exclude?
2. **The reason to buy:** what can we show that supports the offer? Who has approved those examples and claims?
3. **The channel hypothesis:** what evidence suggests these buyers search for the service, respond to a visual explanation, or both?
4. **The costs:** what goes to the platform, and what pays for management, creative, the page and measurement?
5. **The economics:** what does a suitable job contribute after its delivery costs? How much acquisition cost can the business support?
6. **The inquiry:** what makes it qualified, who receives it and how will its outcome be recorded?
7. **The review:** who can pause spend, what is the spending limit and what evidence will support continuing, changing or stopping?

For the questions to put to a prospective provider, use our [Google Ads agency hiring checklist](/articles/hiring-a-google-ads-agency/).

## Decide from outcomes, and investigate before blaming the channel

Separate visits, submitted inquiries, qualified inquiries, appointments and collected sales. Avoid counting the same customer twice when several channels appear in their journey. A cheap click is a cost observation; it does not establish a sale.

If Search attracts requests for the wrong service or area, review the searches, settings, ad and destination. If Meta produces attention without useful inquiries, examine the objective, message, audience, page and measurement. Either pattern may reveal a correctable problem rather than prove the entire platform is unsuitable.

Sparse results remain inconclusive. A broken inquiry path warrants repair; a test reaching its agreed spending limit warrants a decision. More spending is not the automatic answer to either.
